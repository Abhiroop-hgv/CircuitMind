# DARK FRIDAY — the cold open
### Two-minute role-play · Team Satyatma

**What this is:** the setup only. No product appears, nothing is demonstrated,
nobody solves anything. Four people state the piece of the problem they own,
hit the same wall twice — once on today's disruption, once on a new board they
can't even assess — and say what they wish existed.

Abhiroop frames it in ten seconds and picks it up at the end. He does not speak
inside the play.

**Runtime:** 2:15 · ≈ 400 spoken words

**Cast:** Vishnu (Inventory) · Shruti (Production) · Sreephaneesh (Engineering) ·
Aravind (Procurement) · Abhiroop (frames and closes)

---

## FRAMING · 0:00–0:10

> **ABHIROOP** *(camera on, no slide)*
> Ten seconds of setup. An electronics factory, the morning something goes
> wrong. Four people, four systems. Listen for the question none of them can
> answer.

`SCREEN: black. Two words, bottom left — DARK FRIDAY.`

---

## PART ONE — TODAY'S FIRE · 0:10–1:10

> **VISHNU**
> Morning — sorry, has anyone seen this? Trade notice, about an hour old.
> China's put an export licence on microcontroller shipments.

> **ARAVIND**
> Microcontrollers as in —

> **VISHNU**
> The little chips that run the board. Yeah.

> **SHRUTI**
> Okay. Which of our boards?

> **VISHNU**
> *(pause)* I don't know.

> **SHRUTI**
> You don't know?

> **VISHNU**
> I can tell you every chip in that warehouse, to the unit. I can't tell you
> which product it goes into. That's a different system.

> **SHRUTI**
> I've got the other half and it's no better. I know what we've promised
> customers next month. I can't tell you if this chip is what stops us
> shipping it.

> **SREEPHANEESH**
> And I can tell you whether a different chip would do instead — that's my
> job, I can answer by this afternoon. But someone has to tell me which part
> we're talking about first.
> *(beat)*
> And before anyone starts searching — the part numbers look almost
> identical. The chips are not. One letter different and it physically won't
> fit the board. Wrong number of legs, nothing to solder it to. Nobody buys on
> a name that looks close.

> **ARAVIND**
> Then it lands on me. I can buy anything. I need three things — what, how
> many, by when. Right now I've got none of them.

---

## PART TWO — AND IT'S NOT JUST TODAY · 1:10–1:50

> **SHRUTI**
> And this isn't even the only one. Sreephaneesh — the new revision. Are we
> building that next week or not?

> **SREEPHANEESH**
> You tell me. I sent the parts list over on Tuesday.

> **VISHNU**
> You sent me a PDF. Two hundred and forty lines.

> **SREEPHANEESH**
> That's a parts list.

> **VISHNU**
> That's a *picture* of a parts list. To tell you whether we can build it, I
> have to take two hundred and forty rows off a document and check every one
> against stock. By hand.
> *(beat)*
> And half of them aren't even written the way we write them. Same component,
> different name.

> **ARAVIND**
> And whatever you can't match comes to me as "can you source this" — with no
> quantity and no date.

> **SHRUTI**
> So. We don't know what today's notice costs us. And we don't know if we can
> build the new board either.

> **VISHNU**
> Same reason both times.

*(silence — two full seconds)*

---

## THE TURN · 1:50–2:05

> **SHRUTI**
> Between the four of us, we have the whole answer.

> **VISHNU**
> And it takes a week to put together.

> **ARAVIND**
> A week we haven't got.

> **SREEPHANEESH**
> I don't want another system. I've already got four.

> **SHRUTI**
> I want the four we've got to talk to each other. Today. Before the line
> stops.

`SCREEN: hold black for a second. Then cut.`

## HANDOFF · 2:05–2:15

> **ABHIROOP** *(unmutes, camera up)*
> Everything they need already exists. It's sitting in the systems they've all
> been logged into the whole time.
> *(beat)*
> What's missing is the thing that connects it.

*(Presentation continues from here.)*

## NOTES

**Why two fronts.** One crisis reads as bad luck. Two problems with the same
cause read as a structural gap — which is what you're selling. Vishnu's line
**"Same reason both times"** is the hinge of the whole play. Don't cut it, don't
rush it.

**The BOM exchange is the most relatable thing in the script.** Every juror has
been handed a PDF and asked a question it can't answer. *"That's a picture of a
parts list"* does more work than any statistic. And "same component, different
name" quietly plants why matching is hard — which pays off later when you show
unknown parts being ranked for a human instead of auto-matched.

**Sreephaneesh carries the one technical idea the jury must keep:** a
near-identical part number is not an interchangeable part. Legs and solder,
never a part code.

**Beats — four, one second each:**
- after "I don't know" — slightly embarrassed
- after "which part we're talking about first"
- after "By hand"
- the two-second silence after "Same reason both times"

**Overlap the opening.** Vishnu starts before Shruti finishes. Clean turn-taking
is what makes a play sound rehearsed.

**Nobody names the product.** Not once. No AI, no software, no system-that-fixes-
this. They end on a want, not a hint.

**Say the last line flat.** "Before the line stops" is a Friday morning, not a
disaster film.

**If you run long,** cut Aravind's "with no quantity and no date". Everything
else is load-bearing.
